What your camera scope should include

Begin with what each camera needs to show. The site review considers the intended view, mounting position, lighting conditions, cable pathway and network equipment. The written proposal should identify the camera count, supplied equipment, installation responsibilities and any existing-system troubleshooting that is included.

For IP-camera projects, cabling, network ports and recorder setup need to be considered together. CRIT also handles structured cabling and network-closet work, so those requirements can be scoped alongside the camera installation rather than left as a separate surprise.

Installation and handoff

Every project is owner-scoped, technician-installed, tested, documented and owner-reviewed. The agreed handoff can cover camera views, recorder connections, network access and documentation. Confirm any ongoing support or monitoring arrangements separately in the proposal.

Questions to resolve before installation

Can we supply the cameras?

Tell us what you already have, including camera, recorder and network-equipment model numbers. Compatibility, missing components, installation scope and warranty responsibilities need to be confirmed before the work is accepted.

Can existing cable be reused?

Possibly, but its type, condition and suitability for the planned equipment must be reviewed. A replacement camera does not automatically resolve an existing cable or power problem.

What information helps with a quote?

Send the property address, desired camera count, proposed locations, indoor or outdoor requirements, existing equipment models and a preferred schedule. Photographs of the mounting areas and recorder or network closet help explain access and routing.
